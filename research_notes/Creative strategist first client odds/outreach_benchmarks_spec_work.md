# Cold Outreach Benchmarks and Spec Work for a Solo Freelancer Pitching DTC Brands

> **Method note for the report writer:** WebFetch was blocked by the network egress proxy for instantly.ai, belkins.io, lemlist.com, hunter.io and substack.com, so I could not open any primary report. Every figure below comes from search-result summaries and snippets of those pages, or from secondary sites that cite them. The numbers are attributed to the source they are linked to, but **treat each one as second-hand until someone checks it against the primary report.** Almost all benchmark sources are cold email or outreach software vendors (Instantly, Belkins, lemlist, Hunter, Saleshandy, Mailshake, Prospeo and others). They have a commercial interest in showing that cold outreach works and that their features (personalization, video, warmup) lift results. Nothing I found was DTC-specific or about a solo creative strategist.

## 1. Cold email benchmarks for 2024–2026: open, reply, positive reply and meeting rates

### Takeaway
On the big platforms, an average cold email campaign in 2025 got about a 3.4–4.5% reply rate. Only about a quarter to a third of those replies are positive, and a working campaign books roughly 1 meeting per 250–500 sends (0.5–2% of prospects). Reply rates have been falling every year (about 7% in 2023, 5.1% in 2024, 3.43% in 2025). Small, highly targeted sends do 2–3x better than large blasts.

### Cited Findings
- **Instantly 2026 benchmark (2025 sends, Jan–Dec):** the platform-wide average reply rate is **3.43%**, the top quartile reaches **≥5.5%**, and the top 10% reach **≥10.7%**. — [Instantly, AI Sales Agent Benchmarks 2026](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sales-agent-benchmarks-2026-the-complete-performance-report/); also cited in [Instantly, Email Sequence Benchmarks 2026](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/)
- **Downward trend:** average reply rates went from about **7% in 2023** to **about 5.1% in 2024** to **3.43% in 2025**. — [LeadHaste, Outbound Sales Benchmarks 2026](https://www.leadhaste.com/blog/outbound-sales-benchmarks-2026) (secondary, vendor)
- **The first email does most of the work:** in Instantly data, **58% of replies come from the first email** and 42% from follow-ups. — cited by [lemlist, Cold Email Benchmarks 2026](https://lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks/)
- **Hunter State of Cold Email (31M emails sent by Hunter users in 2025):** the average sequence reply rate is **4.5%**, or 45 replies per 1,000 recipients. — [Hunter, The State of Cold Email](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email)
- **Hunter, small vs. large sends:** sequences with **21–50 recipients got a 6.2% reply rate**, compared with **2.4% for 500+ recipients**. Campaigns **without open tracking got 7.4%** versus 4.4% with tracking. Sending from a **custom domain got 5.2%** versus **2.5% from freemail** (gmail.com and similar). — [Hunter, The State of Cold Email 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025) (taken from a search summary; please verify)
- **Belkins 2025 study (16.5M cold emails across 93 business domains, Jan–Dec 2024):** a **single initial email had the highest reply rate, 8.4%**, and reply rates fell as more follow-ups were added. Sequences of **4+ emails substantially increased unsubscribes and spam complaints**. Small and mid-size companies tolerated more follow-ups than enterprises. **Founders' response rates held up through the first follow-up and dropped by the fourth.** C-suite executives replied less than entry-level staff overall. — [Belkins, B2B cold email response rates 2025](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates); [Belkins, Sales follow-up statistics](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics?frame=0)
- **Methodology conflict:** Belkins material published later (2026, by send day) shows email reply figures of only about **0.45–0.57%**, which appears to exclude auto-replies and count differently. For comparison, Saleshandy reports **3.7%**. — [EmailBison, cold email open rates](https://emailbison.com/blogs/cold-email-open-rates); [Belkins, best time to send B2B emails 2026](https://belkins.io/blog/email-deliverability/choosing-the-best-time-to-send-an-email). The benchmarks cannot be compared directly because each vendor defines "reply" differently.
- **Positive reply rate:** Instantly's own metrics guide gives **no benchmark** ("varies by offer"). — [Instantly, meeting email metrics](https://instantly.ai/blog/meeting-email-metrics-to-track-response-booking-rates/). One third-party guide estimates **0.5–1.5% of sends, about ¼–⅓ of all replies**. — [Growthflare, reply-rate benchmarks from 5M+ sends](https://growthflare.io/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/). Another cites **3–5%** from Saleshandy's 2026 study. — [Empra, B2B reply-rate benchmarks 2026](https://www.empra.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks-2026). These two estimates conflict.
- **Meeting rate:** Instantly puts meetings booked per prospect emailed at **0.5–2%** on average in 2025, with top performers above 2%. — [Instantly, meeting email metrics](https://instantly.ai/blog/meeting-email-metrics-to-track-response-booking-rates/). Growthflare puts it at about **1 call per 250–500 sends** on a working campaign. — [Growthflare](https://growthflare.io/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/)
- **Industry variation:** one source says Legal Services gets about 10% replies. Instantly lists nonprofits and religious organizations above 16.5%. — [LeadHaste](https://www.leadhaste.com/blog/outbound-sales-benchmarks-2026); [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/). I found no clean "agency/marketing services selling to ecommerce brands" breakdown.
- **Other 2025–2026 roundups** that aggregate the same vendor data: [Martal, B2B Cold Email Statistics 2026](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics-lb/); [Mailshake, State of Cold Email](https://mailshake.com/blog/the-state-of-cold-email-2025/); [The Digital Bloom, reply-rate benchmarks 2025](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/).

### Inferences
- A sensible baseline for a generic, templated campaign in 2025–2026 is about **2–4% replies, ≤1% positive replies, and about 0.2–0.5% meetings booked per send**. At that rate, landing one client would take thousands of sends, which a solo freelancer with a warmed domain can't send safely in a short period (see Section 5).
- The Hunter finding on small sends (6.2% for 21–50 recipients vs. 2.4% for 500+) and the Belkins finding that a single email gets 8.4% both support a low-volume, highly targeted approach for a solo freelancer.
- The figures are platform averages that include many SaaS and IT senders. Freelancers pitching DTC founders are a different population, so these numbers are directional only.

### Gaps
- I could not open any primary report (egress blocked), and no benchmark broke out "freelancer or agency → DTC/ecommerce founder" as a segment.
- Gong data was not found in this pass. Woodpecker benchmarks were not retrieved.
- I found no reliable **close rate** benchmark (meeting → paid client) for freelance creative or marketing services sold through cold outreach.

## 2. Highly personalized outreach (Loom videos, custom audits, free work first) vs. generic outreach

### Takeaway
Personalization reliably lifts reply rates, roughly from **1.5–2x** (Hunter's custom attributes, 5.6% vs 3.6%) to **2x** (about 18% vs about 9%, vendor-reported). Video and Loom claims run from **+19%** (Intercom, a vendor case study) to **+300%** (unverified). Most of the very high figures (34% or 50%+ reply rates) are anecdotal, small-sample, or come from people selling courses.

### Cited Findings
- **Hunter:** emails with **two custom attributes** in the body got about **5.6% replies vs 3.6% with no personalization**, and **manually edited emails got 5.2% vs 4.4% for automated ones**. — cited via secondary site; [Hunter, State of Cold Email](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email) (the year attribution is unclear, so verify). A separate "**52% higher reply rates** for deep personalization" figure is attributed to a Hunter analysis of 11M emails. — [The Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/) (secondary)
- **Advanced personalization vs generic:** about **18% reply rate for campaigns using advanced personalization vs ~9% for generic templates**, and separately **1–2% for generic emails vs 3–5% for personalized ones**. — [Autobound, Cold Email Guide 2026](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/cold-email-guide-2026); [Martal](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics-lb/) (vendor sources, methodology unclear)
- **Loom / Intercom (Loom's own customer story, about 2022):** Intercom's sales team saw a **19% increase in cold email reply rate** after adding Loom videos, and **$120k** in self-sourced outbound deals. — [Loom, Intercom customer story](https://www.loom.com/customers/intercom) (vendor case study; the relative lift is 19%, not 19 percentage points)
- **Other video claims (unverified):** Prospeo says the average reply rate is 5.1% and that **adding video lifts it to 10–16%**. It also cites SalesLoft research showing video gives **+16% opens and +26% replies**. — [Prospeo, video prospecting scripts](https://prospeo.io/s/video-prospecting-script). Weezly cites an unnamed agency that got "**+300%** replies and leads in two months". — [Weezly](https://weezly.com/blog/boost-cold-email-replies-with-loom-videos/). One cold email agency reports **607 positive replies over 13 months** for a branded apparel client using a Loom pitch, but gives no baseline or volume. — [Icemail](https://icemail.ai/blog/use-loom-video-pitches-cold-email). A Loom-reported "300% increase in response rates" appears only in a secondary Substack write-up. — [Substack issue #35](https://substack.com/home/post/p-158762500)
- **Personalized video audit anecdote (SEO):** one practitioner reportedly sent **150 emails with personalized video audits → 34% reply rate → 8 paying clients → €12K**. — [Prospeo, How to get SEO clients without cold calling](https://prospeo.io/s/how-to-get-seo-clients-without-cold-calling) (vendor blog, single anecdote, unverified). If accurate, that is about **1 client per 19 sends**, which is far above any benchmark.
- **Freelance copywriter anecdote:** after switching from formal, boring first messages to **personalized emails, they get over 50% response rates**. — [Good Copy Tips Substack](https://goodcopytips.substack.com/p/from-inbox-ignored-to-client-magnet) (anecdotal, self-reported)
- **Copyhackers (Bree Weber):** recommends sending to **one recipient at a time and pitching a single specific problem**, and says this raises reply rates. — [Copyhackers, How to Cold Pitch Effectively and Ethically](https://copyhackers.com/how-to-cold-pitch/)
- **Low-end reality check:** an AI agent sent **50 cold emails** to Show HN founders offering an accessibility audit and got **1 positive reply** (2%). — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/claudeprime/i-got-my-first-lead-on-christmas-eve-an-ais-accessibility-audit-journey-2dg6) (not DTC, but a real and unsponsored data point)

### Inferences
- A cautious planning range for **hand-researched, one-to-one outreach with a specific observation or mini-audit** to well-qualified DTC founders: about **8–20% reply, 3–8% positive reply, 2–5% meetings per send**. This is my inference, triangulated from the 2x personalization lift, the 6.2% Hunter small-send figure, the 8.4% Belkins single-email figure, and the top-decile 10.7%. The 34% and 50%+ anecdotes are best-case outliers and should not be used for planning.
- **Funnel math (inference, not sourced):** at 3% meetings per send and a guessed 15–30% meeting-to-close rate for a cheap first offer, the first client would take about **110–220 personalized touches**. With a full spec ad or audit for each prospect at 1–2 hours each, that is **100–400 hours of unpaid work**. A short observation plus an offer of a free teardown on request cuts the cost per touch to about 10–20 minutes. Generic outreach (about 0.3% meetings) would take roughly **1,000–2,500+ sends** for the same result.
- Loom and video lifts in credible sources are modest (+19% to +26% relative). The big lift comes from relevance and specificity (correct ICP, a real observed problem), not from the video format itself.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled data comparing "spec ad attached" vs "observation only" vs "generic" for DTC brands.
- I found no credible meeting → close rates for freelance creative strategists or ad creative services.

## 3. Freelancer case studies: landing first DTC clients through cold outreach with spec work

### Takeaway
I found **no verifiable first-person case study** of a freelancer or creative strategist landing a first DTC client through spec ads plus cold outreach with stated volumes and timelines. The available material is self-reported anecdote or course and Gumroad marketing. One notable signal is that **at least one DTC buyer explicitly rejects spec work as portfolio evidence.**

### Cited Findings
- **A June 2026 Upwork posting for a DTC creative strategist** asks for "a portfolio of ads you actually wrote that ran on paid social — **not spec work**". — [Upwork job posting](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Creative-Strategist-DTC-Performance-Ads-Part-Time-Full-Time_~022064891501473411734/)
- **A freelance email marketer (Kate Citron)** cold-pitches early-stage DTC/CPG brands with no or lean marketing teams. She **audits the brand first** (for example, checking whether it uses Klaviyo and has weak flows) and **skips brands that already have strong flows or aren't on Klaviyo**. She notes that cold outreach works better when you have relevant industry experience and may be weaker for a first client if you're pivoting in from another field. — [Kate Citron Substack](https://katecitron.substack.com/p/how-i-cold-email-my-way-to-new-freelance) (from a search summary; she gives no volume or reply numbers)
- **Klaviyo freelancers commonly use free audits as lead magnets** (a "free 15-minute audit", a "free 108-point audit"), with paid audits or retainers priced at about **$997/mo** or **from £1,450/mo**. — [Upwork Klaviyo freelancers](https://www.upwork.com/hire/klaviyo-freelancers/); [Sendtonium free Klaviyo audit](https://www.sendtonium.com/get-a-free-klaviyo-account-audit)
- **Self-reported first-client claims** on Gumroad and course pages (for example, "**2 clients in 2 weeks** after my first ever cold email", "booked 3 discovery calls") are marketing claims tied to paid products. — [Gumroad example](https://zachnaidoobiz.gumroad.com/l/qjfgj); [Gumroad example](https://codediteam.gumroad.com/l/mofpn)
- **Time to first client through other channels (anecdotal):** a Shopify partner's hand-delivered letter campaign produced a first client **within two weeks**. — [Shopify Partners blog](https://www.shopify.com/partners/blog/the-counter-intuitive-approach-to-getting-your-first-client). A freelance writer landed a first client from Reddit "**within a few weeks**". — [The All Write Letter](https://theallwriteletter.substack.com/p/the-secret-social-media-goldmine)
- **Public free work producing inbound leads:** a team that made **unsolicited redesigns of trending brands and posted them on X** reported inbound calls as a result. — [OnePage Research, distribution pattern](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/lessons/distribution/dist-p327) (single anecdote)
- **Which DTC brands to target:** one DTC guide says brands at around the multi-million revenue stage should make a media buyer or creative strategist their first in-house marketing hire, which suggests that segment needs this skill set. — [MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/dtc-creative-strategist-role/)

### Inferences
- A realistic expectation, inferred and not sourced, is that the first client takes **weeks to a few months** and **roughly 100–300 personalized touches**, with a wide spread depending on niche fit, proof (ads that actually ran), and offer price.
- Because some DTC buyers discount spec ads as portfolio proof, a creative strategist should frame spec work as **"here's what I noticed in *your* account or ads"** (diagnosis) rather than as a portfolio substitute. Getting 1–2 ads that actually ran, even through cheap or paid trial work, may matter more than a large volume of spec work.

### Gaps
- I found no first-person, verifiable stories with send counts, reply counts and timelines for creative strategists or ad freelancers in DTC.
- I found no Upwork or Contra data on time to first client.
- A claimed analysis of "~200k freelance-subreddit posts" saying free work converts fastest and cold email is slowest showed up in one search summary, but I could not locate or verify the underlying source, so it is excluded as evidence.

## 4. Does spec work backfire or work? Best practices

### Takeaway
The evidence is almost entirely opinion. The professional-association view (AIGA, the NO!SPEC tradition) and many freelancer forums say full spec work devalues the work, attracts cheap clients, and often gets no reply. Growth and agency voices (Hormozi-style "give away value", audit lead magnets) say a **scoped, diagnostic freebie** works. The consensus middle ground: give away **the diagnosis, not the deliverable**. Keep it short and specific to the prospect, and make the paid version clearly different.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition and professional stance:** spec work is creative work submitted to prospective clients without an agreed fair fee. AIGA holds that professional designers should be fairly compensated, and the term is closely tied to design-contest critiques. — [Wikipedia, Speculative work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_work)
- **Backfire evidence (anecdotal):** a new freelancer sending **unrequested concept sites got no replies**, and commenters called spec work a trap in a market full of cheap alternatives. — [Yo Mother Board Q&A, "Is spec work a trap for new freelancers?"](https://yomotherboard.com/question/is-spec-work-a-trap-for-new-freelancers/). A UX course argues that spec work "will not ever end well for you". — [UX365, Saying no to spec work](https://ux365.heightsplatform.com/courses/saying-no-to-spec-work-cheapskate-clients/preview) (opinion)
- **Buyer-side signal:** a DTC creative strategist job post explicitly says **"not spec work"** for portfolio evidence. — [Upwork posting](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Creative-Strategist-DTC-Performance-Ads-Part-Time-Full-Time_~022064891501473411734/)
- **"Works" side (opinion):** Hormozi's principle is to look at what competitors charge for and give it away free, making the free item better than others' paid versions. — [ContentGrip, Hormozi acquisition strategies](https://www.contentgrip.com/alex-hormozi-customer-acquisition-strategies/); [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/alex-hormozi-business-lessons/) (secondary summaries)
- **Middle ground:** use a **free mini-audit as a lead magnet with the full audit as a paid upsell**, stating the difference clearly. Audit-led outreach tools emphasize presenting the **findings** rather than a finished deliverable. — [Product Hunt listing](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/1234799); [Fiverr community blog](https://community.fiverr.com/public/blogs/10-profitable-ways-to-promote-your-freelance-business)
- **Alternatives to spec work:** pitch guides recommend showing **1–2 existing samples** and personalizing to the client's problem rather than producing custom unpaid work. — [Inkwell Academy](https://inkwellacademy.substack.com/p/5-templates-to-land-your-first-copywriting); [Wild Writing, warm pitching](https://wildwriting.substack.com/p/the-power-of-warm-pitching)
- **Follow-up cadence:** Belkins found that **one email got the highest reply rate (8.4%)**, that **4+ email sequences raise spam complaints and unsubscribes**, and that founders stop responding by the 4th touch. — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates). Instantly found **58% of replies come from email #1**. — cited by [lemlist](https://lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- **Best-practice synthesis (inference):**
  1. Lead with a **2–3 sentence specific observation** about the brand's ads, site or emails, plus an offer: "want the 3-minute Loom?" Don't attach a full spec ad set up front.
  2. If you do send work, keep it **small**: one Loom teardown of 2–4 minutes, or **one or two** concept ads or hooks, with the reasoning shown.
  3. **Hold back execution** (finished, ready-to-launch assets) for paid work.
  4. Use **2–3 total touches** at most, spaced several days apart.
  5. Optionally **publish teardowns publicly** (X, LinkedIn) so the free work also builds inbound and proof.
- The risk of ideas being stolen is real but small in scale: a single hook or concept can be copied for free. That supports sharing the diagnosis and strategic reasoning, which signals expertise, rather than a polished, ready-to-run creative.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative study comparing spec-work outreach with no-spec-work outreach.
- I found no data on how often prospects use pitched ideas without hiring.

## 5. Deliverability factors that make outreach "not work"

### Takeaway
A solo freelancer's outreach can fail before anyone reads it for several reasons: sending from a brand-new, unwarmed domain; blasting volume; missing SPF/DKIM/DMARC; tracking pixels; or spam complaints above 0.1–0.3%. Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 sender rules and practitioner norms (a secondary domain, 2–4 weeks of warmup, about 30 sends per inbox per day) cap realistic volume.

### Cited Findings
- **Gmail/Yahoo rules (from Feb 2024):** bulk senders (about **5,000+ messages/day** to Gmail or Yahoo) must authenticate with **SPF, DKIM and DMARC**, offer **one-click unsubscribe** on marketing mail, and keep **spam complaints below 0.3%**. Some sources say the 0.3% limit applies to **all** senders, so sources disagree here. The recommended target is **<0.1%**. — [Courier](https://www.courier.com/blog/email-sender-requirements); [Bird](https://bird.com/docs/knowledge-base/deliverability/gmail-yahoo-requirements); [OneSignal](https://documentation.onesignal.com/docs/en/new-google-and-yahoo-email-updates.md); [3MediaWeb](https://www.3mediaweb.com/blog/new-google-yahoo-bulk-email-requirements-now-in-effect/)
- **Authentication alignment:** publishing the three DNS records is not enough on its own. SPF or DKIM must pass **aligned with the From domain**. Non-compliant mail now gets **permanent SMTP rejections** (Gmail 5.7.x; Outlook 550 5.7.515). — [Bird](https://bird.com/explained/deliverability/what-are-the-gmail-and-yahoo-bulk-sender-requirements)
- **Warmup:** building reputation typically takes **2–4 weeks**, starting at **5–10 emails/day**. Other sources say to age new domains at least **2 weeks**, or to start at **10–15/day and add 5–10 every few days**. — [Warmforge](https://warmforge.ai/blog/cold-email-deliverability-guide-2025); [Databar](https://databar.ai/blog/article/email-domain-warm-up-the-complete-guide-for-maximum-deliverability-in-2025); [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/deliverability-audit-to-improve-email-deliverability) (vendor heuristics)
- **Per-inbox caps:** **25–30/day** is the most common conservative ceiling after warmup. Other figures are "never exceed 50 (30 safer for new inboxes)" and Mailshake's **40–60/day** in high-volume setups. Salesforge calls the "50 max" rule a myth. — [Mailshake, Secondary Domains](https://mailshake.com/blog/secondary-domains-the-essential-guide-for-safe-scaling/); [LeadHaste, how many domains](https://leadhaste.com/blog/how-many-domains-do-you-need-for-cold-email); [Salesforge](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/unlocking-pipeline-growth-with-warmed-up-emails)
- **Secondary domains:** do cold outreach from **secondary domains, never the primary**, with 2+ inboxes per domain. Mailshake suggests **1–2 domains × 2 inboxes for up to about 3,000 emails/month**. — [Mailshake](https://mailshake.com/blog/secondary-domains-the-essential-guide-for-safe-scaling/)
- **Hunter data:** replies from a **custom domain were 5.2% vs 2.5% from freemail**, and **no open tracking got 7.4% vs 4.4% with tracking**. — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)

### Inferences
- A solo freelancer sending 20–40 hand-personalized emails a day is well under bulk thresholds and per-inbox caps, so **effort, not deliverability, is the binding limit** for spec-work outreach. Freelancers still need SPF/DKIM/DMARC, a custom domain, ideally a secondary sending domain warmed for 2+ weeks, open tracking turned off, and no heavy attachments. Linking to a Loom or hosted file is generally safer than attaching files, but that is a practitioner norm I did not source.
- Weak results in the first 2 weeks from a new domain may come from deliverability problems rather than the message. Seed testing or placement checks are advisable (not sourced).

### Gaps
- I found no official Google or Microsoft numeric guidance on warmup or per-inbox limits. All figures are vendor heuristics.

## 6. Reaching founders: generic inboxes vs named founder emails vs Instagram DMs vs LinkedIn

### Takeaway
I found **no data directly comparing generic inboxes (hello@, support@) with named founder emails**. Instagram DM benchmarks are vendor-published and inconsistent: about 5–10% replies for generic DMs and 15–30% for DMs that reference the recipient's content. DMs have low safe daily limits, and founders reply to email at stable rates through one follow-up.

### Cited Findings
- **Instagram DMs:** **15–25% reply rate for well-crafted cold DMs to ICP targets vs 5–10% for generic DMs**. Another source gives **5–10% for generic and 15–30% when the DM references the recipient's content**. Higher figures (50–60%) apply to **engaged/warm audiences** and are not cold benchmarks. — [Phantom, Cold DM vs Cold Email for agencies](https://phantomleads.ai/compare/cold-dm-vs-cold-email); [Phantom, Cold outreach statistics 2026](https://phantomleads.ai/blog/cold-outreach-statistics-2026); [Creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-dm-metrics-that-matter/); [xAutoDM, Cold DM benchmarks 2026](https://xautodm.com/blog/cold-dm-benchmarks-reply-rates-that-are-actually-good-2026) (all vendors of DM tools, so bias is likely)
- **DM volume limits:** Instagram allows about **20–80 DMs/day** safely, compared with thousands of emails. — [Phantom](https://phantomleads.ai/compare/cold-dm-vs-cold-email)
- **Founder behaviour:** founders' email response rates **held steady through the first follow-up, then dropped by the fourth**. C-suite executives reply less than junior staff overall. — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
- **Finding the right contact:** freelance email marketers check the brand's own marketing emails, for example to identify the ESP (Klaviyo) from links, before pitching. — [Kate Citron Substack](https://katecitron.substack.com/p/how-i-cold-email-my-way-to-new-freelance)
- **Generic vs named contacts:** I found no source that quantifies info@/hello@ vs named-founder reply rates. — (gap; see below)

### Inferences
- For small DTC brands, the founder often runs the Instagram account or reads its DMs, and a DM referencing their ads can work. DM benchmarks are vendor numbers, though. A multichannel approach (a personalized email to the named founder plus a light-touch IG or LinkedIn interaction) is a common practitioner recommendation but was **not** quantified in the sources I found.
- Generic inboxes at DTC brands are often staffed by customer support. Pitches sent there probably perform worse than named-founder emails. This is a reasonable inference with no data behind it.

### Gaps
- I found no data on reply rates for hello@/support@ inboxes vs a named founder.
- I found no LinkedIn InMail or connection-message reply benchmarks specific to DTC founders. lemlist's multichannel uplift figures could not be retrieved because the site was blocked.
