## 2. The promise (what the brand stands for)

**The change we seek** (Seth: "who's it for, what's it for"):

> Help ambitious men keep building, without paying for it with their body, their marriage or their peace.

**The promise:**

> Project 1% tells you the truth: about what success costs, about the numbers, about the price. Then it gives you
> one thing to do today.

**What that rules in:** real numbers (including the ugly ones), one consistent story, real dates, real member
counts, a real price, a guarantee, plain language, Richard's scars as well as his wins.

**What that rules out:** fake scarcity, value-stack theatre, "secrets passed down in back rooms", lifestyle flexing,
insulting the viewer, inflated movement claims, any number that changes between touchpoints.

**Positioning statement (draft):**

> For ambitious men in the build years, who are starting to feel what it's costing them, Project 1% is a 35-day
> training that installs a personal code across body, money, relationships and mind. Unlike hustle challenges
> that test your willpower, or single-pillar coaches who fix one thing while the others slide, it's built by a man
> ten years ahead of you who paid the full price for success and shows you the receipt.

**Lines to test** (each one sticks to the promise):

- "Build the man before you build the number."
- "Keep the ambition. Skip the invoice."
- "Don't wait for the slipped disc."
- "Your scorecard has one line on it. Add eight."
- "I paid for it with two discs and a 7½-year relationship. You don't have to."

## 4. Positioning map (Module 6)

Seth's rule is to choose two axes where **both ends are good things**, so you can honestly send the wrong buyer
somewhere else.

- **Axis 1: Specialist ↔ Integrator.** Master one pillar deeply, or build one system across all of them.
- **Axis 2: Intensity ↔ Sustainability.** A hard test of will, or principles that run on bad days.

```
                     INTENSITY (a hard test of will)
                               ▲
   75 Hard · Goggins           │        Wake Up Warrior
   Hormozi (money)             │        (married businessmen, 4 Bs,
   Jocko                       │         $10k events, faith-led, high-pressure)
                               │
 SPECIALIST ───────────────────┼─────────────────────────► INTEGRATOR
                               │
   Huberman · Attia (health)   │        ★ PROJECT 1%
   Gottman (marriage)          │        (builders mid-climb, one code,
   Arthur Brooks (happiness,   │         one lesson a day, a brotherhood,
   books and talks)            │         by a man 10 years ahead)
                               ▼
                     SUSTAINABILITY (runs on bad days)
```

**Richard's corner: Integrator × Sustainable, for men still building.** Wake Up Warrior is integrator and intense
(and now explicitly Christian). Huberman and Attia are sustainable, but cover one pillar. Brooks has the ideas but no
system, community or money pillar.

**The generous referral, as ad copy** (the Volvo/Ferrari move):

> If you want to get shredded in 12 weeks, hire a fitness coach.
> If you want to scale to eight figures, go follow Hormozi.
> If you want a brutal 75-day test of willpower, do 75 Hard. It's great.
> If you want to keep building and stop paying for it with your back, your marriage and your sleep, that's what
> Project 1% is for.

This replaces the "simp / cynic" filter. It does the same job (repels the wrong man), but generously.

## 6. Offer and pricing

**First, the job of the front end.** The business model is a front end that leads to a strategy call, which leads
to high-ticket coaching (Richard says men have paid him $25k a year for 1:1). So the front end has two jobs:
attract the right man, and earn the right to the call. A $9 price does the first job for the wrong man, and the
confirmation-page call push skips the second.

### Option A: your idea, "$9 for your first month"

- **Shape:** $9 for the first 35 days (the full Foundation), then the Brotherhood continues at $X a month, cancel
  with one email. The full monthly price is shown in the ad, on the page and at checkout.
- **Good:** low risk for the buyer, recurring revenue that pays for the community, and the price is honest if
  it's stated up front.
- **Watch out for:**
  - "Month" against a 35-day program and a 30-day guarantee is a mismatch. Line them up: 35 days, then a 30-day
    guarantee that runs from day 35.
  - A $9 trial is the app-store genre. It only reads as premium if the ongoing price is clearly premium.
  - A trial that converts automatically and quietly breaks the honesty promise. It must be loud and clear.

### Option B: founding cohort, real price, real date (my recommendation for the core offer)

- **Shape:** the 35-day Foundation runs as a cohort starting on a fixed date (for example, the first Monday of each
  month). It has a real price with the 30-day guarantee, a real cap tied to something Richard really does (for
  example, he reads every man's ten rules and runs one live call a week per cohort, so 50 men), and a published
  next-cohort price.
- **After day 35:** the Brotherhood is a monthly membership **only for men who finished**. Earned, not bought. That's
  the status ratchet from Module 4.
- **Calls:** offered on day 35 to finishers. Finishing is the qualification. Ask net worth there, not before $9.
- **Price to test:** two points, for example $97 and $297, against the current $9. Judge on finish rate and the
  quality of calls booked, not on cost per sale alone.
- **Good:** the price signals seriousness; the date creates real tension; finishing becomes status; and the call
  becomes earned, not pushed.
- **Watch out for:** a lower front-end conversion rate. That's the trade.

### Option C: a free front door (pairs with A or B)

- **Shape:** a free 7-day "Write Your Code" email series (or WhatsApp). It covers the ten rules, the nine-line
  scorecard and one action a day, then invites him to the next cohort.
- **Why:** this is Seth's permission marketing (Module 2), and it plays to your strength (email). It builds a list
  Richard owns, so cold traffic stops being the only engine.

**Recommendation:**

- **Run B as the core offer, with C as the front door.**
- **If Richard wants to keep a low entry price, run A against B as a planned test (Module 5):** a coherent
  hypothesis, a fixed budget, a decision date, and judgement on finish rate and qualified calls rather than clicks
  or cost per sale.

## 8. The story he tells (Module 3 and Module 6)

Make Project 1% something a man can explain to his wife, and something his friends ask about.

- **The ten rules (day 1).** A physical or digital card with his own ten rules. He can show it at home. That's the
  "nice shoes, where'd you get them?" moment.
- **The finisher's coin (day 35).** Military culture, which this man respects. "What's that coin?" is a
  conversation.
- **"Bring a brother"** (Seth's 12-pack). A finisher can sponsor one man into the next cohort, which makes him the
  generous leader.
- **The spouse angle.** Content about "what my wife noticed on day 12". She becomes an advocate, not a sceptic.
- **The wording.** "Training", "cohort", "code", "operating system". Never "course", "healing" or "self-help". He
  needs to be able to say it at a barbecue.

## 9. Rollout: smallest viable audience first

1. **Founding cohort from permission, not cold traffic.** Invite the men who fit the persona from the property
   client list, the YouTube subscribers and the email list. Seth sold Purple Cow to readers who already trusted
   him. Target 30–50 men.
2. **Run cohort 1 for proof.** Before-and-after stories, finish rate, and men's words in their own voice.
3. **Then cold ads**, built from cohort 1's language and faces, pointing at the next real cohort date.
4. **Then the high-ticket offer**, to finishers who want more.

## 10. What to measure (not clicks)

| Measure | Why |
|---|---|
| Day-1 completion (ten rules written) | The first proof he's in |
| Day-35 completion rate | The promise kept, and the strongest predictor of everything after it |
| Weekly active members in the Brotherhood | Affiliation is real |
| Refund rate | An honest check on the promise |
| "Brought a brother" rate | The network effect (Module 4) |
| Qualified calls per 100 finishers, then close rate | The business outcome |
| Video play rate, page-to-checkout, cost per sale | Constraints to keep healthy, not the goal (Module 6, "false proxies") |

## 11. Decisions Richard has to make before any new creative

1. **Who:** confirm the primary persona (the self-made builder, 32–45, mid-climb).
2. **The promise:** confirm the honesty promise, and accept everything it rules out.
3. **One fact sheet:** every number in his story, verified, used everywhere.
4. **One framework:** the pillars, the day-1 exercise, and the names, fixed.
5. **The offer shape:** A, B or B plus C, and the cohort calendar.
6. **His own facts:** kids or not, and how much faith goes in the brand.
