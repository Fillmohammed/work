# Repositioning the $9: from "cheap course" to "the $9 bet"

Mohammed's direction (11 Oct 2026): keep $9 for now, but reposition it. Compare $9 to what he already spends $9 on,
let him try it for 35 days, and show the best case and the worst case. A higher price may be pitched to Richard
later.

This document turns that into an offer that fits the persona (`03-primary-persona.md`), the positioning
(`02-repositioning-richard.md`) and Seth's tests.

---

## 1. Why $9 isn't working as positioned today

- **It arrives as a surprise.** Every ad says "less than $97", and the checkout says $9. To a sceptic, a sudden 90%
  drop reads as "what's the catch?", not "great deal".
- **The $5,626 value stack makes it worse.** "Worth $5,626, yours for $9" is the guru genre this man distrusts.
- **For this persona, $9 signals low value.** He pays $9 for things he doesn't think about.
- **Seth's spouse test fails.** The only story left is "it was only $9", which is Seth's "the same thing but cheaper"
  fallback.

**The fix isn't the number. It's what the number means.**

---

## 2. The reframe: $9 isn't the price, it's the stake

This persona thinks like an investor. He judges every deal the same way: **how much can I lose, and how much can I
make?** Richard teaches this himself. His sales video names a lesson "the 10x asymmetrical formula: how to
structure your risk so every bet… can pay you far more than it can ever cost you."

So Project 1% at $9 is positioned as **the most lopsided bet he'll be offered this year, and the only one on
himself.**

> **$9 is the stake. The real price is 35 mornings.**

That one line fixes the status problem:

- **Low entry, high standard.** The checkout already has a commitment contract ("one a day, in order, not binged";
  "show up in the Brotherhood, not as a lurker"). Keep it, and make it the point: "It costs $9 to get in. It costs
  35 days of keeping your word to get through."
- **Finishing is the status, not paying.** The men who finish earn the Brotherhood (see the repositioning document).
- **The story for his partner** becomes: "It's a $9 bet with Richard, the property guy. Worst case I'm out two
  coffees. I've written my ten rules. Hold me to them."

## 3. "What else does $9 buy you?"

His $9 moments, to set the bet against. Use the ones that fit the market (US, UK, Dubai):

- Two flat whites on the way to the office
- The airport coffee and protein bar before a 6am flight
- Twenty minutes of parking at the client meeting
- The Uber surge he didn't notice
- One month of the app he forgot to cancel
- The tip on a business lunch

**The idea behind the list:**

> You spent $9 this morning without thinking about it.
> You spend thousands a year maintaining your car, your house and your portfolio.
> This is the first $9 you'll spend maintaining the asset that runs all of them.

The last line ties back to the big idea from the repositioning document: compounding money while depreciating
yourself.

## 4. Worst case and best case (the core of the offer)

This belongs on the page, near the price, and works as a static ad or as the close of a founder video.

| | **Worst case** | **Best case** |
|---|---|---|
| **Money** | You're out $9, and you can get that back (see the guarantee below) | You find out where your money was quietly costing you |
| **Time** | 35 short lessons. Most are shorter than your commute (Richard's own claim). | The evenings and weekends you stop losing |
| **Body** | You've written down what your body needs | You're training without your back paying for it |
| **Home** | You've written ten rules your partner can read | She notices you're actually in the room |
| **Head** | You learn one way that doesn't work for you | You stop feeling like a treadmill with better carpet |
| **What you keep either way** | Your ten rules, your scorecard, and the lessons you watched | All of that, plus a room of men building alongside you |

**The one-line version:**

> **Worst case: you lose a coffee. Best case: you never end up where I did, on the floor of a $1.5M apartment
> with no one to call.**

(Use the apartment figure that's agreed in the fact sheet.)

## 5. Make the guarantee match the bet

**The problem:** a 30-day guarantee on a 35-day program means he can't finish before the window closes. And "by
staying past 30 days I'm committed" turns day 30 into a cliff. That doesn't match "try it for 35 days".

**Proposal:** a completion-based guarantee.

> **Do all 35 days. If nothing has changed, email us by day 45 and we'll refund your $9, no questions asked.**

Why it works:

1. **It matches your framing exactly:** "try it for 35 days; if it doesn't work, you've lost nothing."
2. **It rewards finishing,** which is the number that matters most (day-35 completion).
3. **It sounds confident.** Richard isn't hiding behind fine print.
4. **The financial risk is tiny.** It's $9.

(Keep "no questions asked" for anyone who wants out early as well. It's $9. Generosity is cheap here and it builds
trust.)

## 6. Say *why* it's $9, honestly

Seth: consistency, and the promise is honesty. So say the reason out loud. Draft lines in Richard's voice, for him
to adjust:

> "One-to-one with me costs $25,000 a year. Most men will never need that. I'd rather you judge me by lesson seven
> than by my sales page, so I made the first 35 days cost less than your coffee order."

> "After you start, my team will offer you a free call. Take it or don't. The 35 days are yours either way."

The second line discloses the strategy call up front. That fixes the "call that appears from nowhere" problem in
the audit (`07-consistency-audit-ad-to-finish.md`, item 6).

## 7. Take out everything that fights the bet

The bet framing only works if nothing else on the journey contradicts it. Remove or change:

| Remove | Why | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| "Less than $97" in the ads and video | It contradicts $9 | "$9" everywhere, from the first second |
| The $5,626 value stack and the $2,997 / $997 / $297 drop-down | "Worth thousands, yours for $9" is the guru genre | One honest anchor: "1:1 with me is $25k a year" |
| "First 100 men, then the price goes up / the offer comes down" | Stress, not tension. It also contradicts "$9 one-time" on the about page. | A real cohort start date if you run cohorts. Otherwise no scarcity at all. The asymmetry is the urgency. |
| "Limited time" (checkout) | Vague and unexplained | Remove it, or replace it with the real cohort date |
| The net-worth question before $9 | It contradicts "a man with almost nothing can get in" | Ask it on the call-booking form |

## 10. How to test it (and what decides it)

**Test 1: framing, at the same $9.**

- Control: the current ad and page copy (with "less than $97" corrected to $9).
- Variant: the bet framing, the worst/best-case table and the completion guarantee.

**Measure, in this order:**

1. Day-35 finish rate
2. Refund rate
3. Calls booked, and how many actually show up
4. Calls that close
5. Page-to-checkout and cost per purchase, as constraints, not as goals

**Optimise Meta on Purchase**, and confirm the Purchase event fires. Today only AddToCart and Schedule are scripted on
the page.

**Later: price.** Once the first cohorts give proof, test the same bet framing at a higher stake (for example $97).
"Worst case, you lose $97" still works for this man. Use finish rate and call quality to decide, not front-end
cost per sale alone.
