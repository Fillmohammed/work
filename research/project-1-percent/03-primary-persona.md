# Project 1%: the primary persona (smallest viable audience)

Built from: the live Project 1% funnel, Richard's true story bank (`04-richard-story-bank.md`), 35 threads from
Blind (an anonymous forum for verified tech and finance employees), BiggerPockets investor threads, public
survey data and a competitor review. Following Module 3, it starts from **worldview** (what he believes before we
arrive) and treats demographics as a consequence, not the definition.

---

## 1. Who he is, in one line

> **The builder with something to lose.** A self-made man who has spent ten years compounding his money and
> depreciating himself, and can now feel it in his back, his marriage and his sleep.

---

## 2. His worldview (what he believes before Richard shows up)

This is the part that decides whether he buys. Every one of these is a belief Richard already shares or once shared.

1. **"I'm a builder."** He made what he has. No inheritance, no shortcuts. He respects people who've done it, and
   distrusts people who just talk about it.
2. **"Own, don't rent."** He thinks like an investor: assets over liabilities, compounding over quick wins. Many have
   bought property or built a business. He'd never let a rental fall into disrepair.
3. **"Effort fixes everything."** It always has. That's why this problem confuses him. More effort is making it worse.
4. **"Fix it after the number."** The gym, the trip, being present: all scheduled for after the next milestone. The
   milestone keeps moving.
5. **"Men handle it."** He doesn't talk about it. When men do say it out loud, other men mock them ("touch grass",
   "buy a convertible"). So he says it in anonymous forums, or nowhere.
6. **"Therapy isn't for me, but a good mentor is."** He's paid for mentors, courses and rooms before. He'll pay
   again, if it's practical and comes from someone who's been there.
7. **"Most gurus are full of it."** He's watched the rented Lamborghinis get exposed. When a rich man says "money
   doesn't buy happiness" he hears: "usually said right after making a ton of it."
8. **"My family is the reason I do this."** He's working this hard *for* them, and he's starting to see that they're
   paying for it.

**The tension inside his worldview:** he believes in compounding and maintenance for every asset except himself.
That's where Project 1% meets him.

## 3. Where his pain, fear and dreams live

Seth: "where are we finding our pain and our fear and our dreams?"

### Pain (his words, from the threads)

- "Got the role, got the money, got the title. But I feel nothing… a treadmill with better carpet."
- "My entire identity feels like [my job]… golden handcuffs and financial mindset (I grew up poor) have me scared to
  pull the trigger."
- "Dad to two beautiful children… feel hollow from inside… About to turn 40." Reply: "more money just accumulates
  in the bank, doesn't cover the loneliness."
- "I'm 35. Feels like 40… I'm trading my good years for good money."
- "Male, married with kids. Early 40s. I don't have any friends." "It's usually the moms… the dads barely talk to one
  another."
- "Always made kids' school events but didn't do simple things like walking them to school… worked in evenings
  instead of playing Lego… Big regrets."
- "I want a life where my kids get more than just my leftover energy."
- From a self-made property investor: "despite the tremendous success I've had… steadily growing more and more
  unhappy with it."

### Fear

- **The break:** the back, the shoulder, the heart scare, the blood test. (Testosterone prescribing for US men aged
  35–44 rose about 45–58% from 2018 to 2022.)
- **Losing the family he's working for.** Women initiate about 69% of US divorces. "You see your kids every second
  weekend by agreement."
- **Turning into his dad**, or into the 45-year-old with $6M who wrote "I was more happy and confident as a student
  with 30k debt."
- **Being the only one who feels this.** The relief when he finds he isn't: "It's not just me. Let's start a club,
  fellas!"

### Dreams

- To feel as good as he looks on paper.
- A body that still works at 60. Floor time with his kids that his back doesn't pay for.
- A partner who respects him, and to actually be in the room.
- A few men he can call at 11pm.
- To keep the ambition, but stop paying for it.

Richard's story contains every one of these moments, which is why he can talk to all of them truthfully.

| **Burnout or a business wobble** | "I can't keep this pace." | 3am Heathrow runs, then the 2025 collapse |

**For early ads:** the injury and the quiet-marriage segments have the most urgency and the clearest story match.

## 7. What he'll tell his partner and his mates (Module 6)

He needs a story he's proud to say out loud. Seth: without it, the only story left is "it was cheap."

- **To his partner:** "I'm doing a 35-day thing with Richard, the property guy. Day one I wrote my ten rules. Here they
  are." She can read them. She can see whether he keeps them. That's his milk carton on the desk: "let's talk about
  this."
- **To his mates:** "You know how we're all running ourselves into the ground? There's a group of guys doing this
  properly. Come in with me." That's the 12-pack: inviting another man makes him the leader, and makes his own life
  better if his friend or business partner changes too.
- **The words he can use:** training, cohort, the code, the scorecard, the foundation. Never "a course", "self-help" or
  "healing".

## 9. Why this man, and not "rich and empty at 50"

1. **He's Richard's mirror.** Same worldview, same stage of life, same breaks. Credibility comes from "people like us",
   and the 7–10 year gap is the believable "ten years ahead" gap.
2. **The offer only makes sense to him.** "Keep building, but build the man first" is for men still building, with
   something to protect.
3. **He already pays for mentorship.** Richard's property buyers are proof: professionals and owners, roughly 30–60,
   who paid for education.
4. **Richard already has permission with him.** Years of property clients, leads, subscribers and email lists. That's
   the start Seth describes in Module 4.
5. **It's a group, not a bunch.** Builders with something to lose respond to one story: "I compounded my money and
   depreciated myself. Here's how I rebuilt."

The rich-and-empty 50-year-old is a valid later audience, reached through referrals and the high-ticket offer, but
he's not the beachhead.
