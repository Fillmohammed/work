Uses Seth Godin's modules (`01-seth-godin-modules-digest.md`), the persona (`03-primary-persona.md`) and Richard's
true stories (`04-richard-story-bank.md`). Following Module 5 ("we make decisions"), this document decides. Where
something needs testing, it's set up as a test with a clear lesson either way.

## 2. What Richard can do that the giants can't

Strategy card: "You can't out Amazon Amazon… there are lots of things you can do that Amazon can't… go find one of
those things and claim it."

The giants of men's self-improvement are Hormozi, Huberman, Attia, Goggins, Tony Robbins, Steven Bartlett, and
(within the "have it all" niche) Garrett White's Wake Up Warrior. Richard can't out-scale, out-science or
out-tough them. Here's what they can't do and he can:

1. **Be mid-journey.** They're finished products speaking from the summit. Richard's rebuild is happening now: the
   2025 collapse, the shoulder surgery, a relationship ended, thinking about becoming a father. His own mentor gave
   him the line: *"Sometimes the biggest advantage isn't showing up with everything already finished. People like
   being part of the journey."*
2. **Be close.** They can't read a member's ten rules or get on a call with a cohort of 50. Richard can, for now. Being
   reachable is the advantage of being small.
3. **Speak money as an owner.** Most men's coaches made their money selling coaching. Richard built a property
   portfolio from a Help to Buy deposit, paid private investors 8–15% a year for a decade, and lives on assets. He can
   talk about the whole life in the language this man already respects: assets, maintenance, depreciation,
   compounding, foundations.
4. **Show all three breaks, lived at a high level.**
   - **Body:** national-level powerlifter, then broken, then rebuilt (real photos).
   - **Money:** council estate to multimillionaire.
   - **Family:** 7½ years as a partner and stepfather, then lost it.
   Few people in the category can tell all three honestly.
5. **Be the outsider.** A British council-estate kid now in Dubai, speaking to an international audience in a feed full
   of American gym-bro and executive voices.
6. **Say "I'm not the biggest name."** Giants can't say that. For this persona, it's disarming.

**How this differs from Wake Up Warrior** (same space, done differently, which is what you described):

| | Wake Up Warrior | Project 1% |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | From the summit. Garrett as general. | From the road, ten years ahead |
| Format | Intense events (Warrior Week, about $10k) and annual dues | 35 days, one lesson a day, a cohort, an earned brotherhood |
| Wealth lens | Business revenue | Ownership and assets ("own, don't rent") |
| Faith | Faith-led ("Warriors for Christ") | Faith-friendly, not faith-led (Richard's call) |
| Who | Married businessmen | Builders 32–48: owners, investors, sales leaders, with partners or kids or stepkids |
| Price feel | High-ticket from the door | An accessible entry that's real; high-ticket earned later |

## 3. The positioning map (axes where both ends are good)

Seth: choose axes "where either axis is a good thing", find the under-inhabited corner, claim it.

- **Horizontal: one game ↔ the whole life.** Concentrating on one pillar is right when you have nothing to lose (a
  24-year-old should go all-in on money). The whole life is right when you have something to protect. Both are good.
- **Vertical: from the summit ↔ from the road.** The summit gives certainty, scale and polish (a finished master).
  The road gives closeness, relevance and honesty (a man ten years ahead, still building). Both are good.

```
                         FROM THE SUMMIT
                  (finished, polished, at scale)
                               ▲
   Hormozi (money)             │      Tony Robbins (events)
   Goggins · 75 Hard (will)    │      Wake Up Warrior (married businessmen)
   Huberman · Attia (health)   │      Arthur Brooks (ideas)
                               │
 ONE GAME ─────────────────────┼─────────────────────────► THE WHOLE LIFE
                               │
   Transformation creators     │      ★ RICHARD NORRIS / PROJECT 1%
   (one pillar, in public)     │      ten years ahead, rebuilding in public,
   Build-in-public founders    │      with the receipts, for builders
                               ▼
                          FROM THE ROAD
             (close, current, still building)
```

**The corner:** the whole life, from the road. It's thinly inhabited, and nobody there has Richard's receipts.

**The Volvo/Ferrari move (generous referral)**, for ads, page and video:

> If you're 24 and have nothing to lose, go all-in on one thing. That's the right move. Follow Hormozi.
> If you want a brutal test of willpower, do 75 Hard. It's a great challenge.
> If you want the deep science of your body, Huberman and Attia are brilliant.
> If you're a married businessman who wants a high-intensity, faith-led brotherhood, Wake Up Warrior built exactly
> that.
> But if you've built something worth protecting, and you can feel it slipping (your back, your marriage, your
> sleep), I'm ten years ahead of you on that road. Come and walk the next part with me.

(Names work in long-form content. In paid ads, use the categories rather than names: "a money guru", "a 75-day
challenge".)

## 4. The big idea: the investor's lens on the whole man

This is how Richard's real strength (property and ownership) becomes the distinctive voice of Project 1%.

> **"I spent ten years compounding my money and depreciating myself."**

Language that comes naturally to him and lands with this persona:

- "You'd never let a rental fall apart the way you've let yourself."
- "Your body is the one asset you can't refinance."
- "Maintenance is cheap. Repairs are not." (His own line: "prevention is cheap and cure is not.")
- "You've got a portfolio statement for your money. What does the one for your marriage say?"
- "Never put money into things that rust, rot or depreciate." (His mentor's rule, now applied to time and attention.)
- "You don't build on a cracked foundation." (The program is already called **the Foundation**.)

**One framework, used everywhere** (a proposal; Richard decides the final names):

- **The Foundation** = you: identity, self-leadership, your **Code** (ten rules written on day one).
- **The four walls it holds up** = Body, Wealth, Love, Peace.
- **The Scorecard** = how you measure all of it weekly, instead of one line called "net worth" (anti-false-proxy).
- **The Brotherhood** = the men building alongside you.

## 5. The promise, and the Nike-hotel test

**The promise:** *the truth about what success costs, from a man who paid it, and one thing to do about it today.*

Module 5: we don't need Richard to "be authentic". We need every touchpoint to keep this promise, every time.

**If Richard Norris opened…**

- **…a gym:** no mirrors-and-ego culture. Men in their 30s and 40s training to be durable, not just strong. The week's
  scorecard on a whiteboard. Everyone gone by 7:30 for the school run.
- **…a retreat:** a property walk-through in the morning, a hard session at lunch, a long dinner with phones in a box,
  and straight talk about money, bodies and marriages. No fire-walking.
- **…a book:** *Compounding Money, Depreciating Myself*, his ledger: what he built, what it cost, what he'd do
  differently.

If we can answer these instantly, it's a brand. Everything below is built to make these answers obvious.

## 6. Genre: from guru funnel to documented rebuild

Module 6: "genre means what do I expect?" Changing the genre changes what he expects, what he'll pay and whether he
trusts it.

| | Guru funnel (today) | Documented rebuild plus founding cohort (proposed) |
|---|---|---|
| **Look** | Black and blood red, blinking, stencil type | Calm and editorial, like a letter from a man |
| **The proof** | Lifestyle and value stacks | Receipts: the photos, the scars, the real numbers, the members |
| **Content** | One 25-minute video, gated | A weekly "rebuild log": what Richard did this week across the four walls, with honest numbers (training, bloodwork, a hard conversation, a deal) |
| **The offer** | A $9 course "for the first 100 men" | A founding cohort that starts on a date |
| **Ads** | Polished founder videos and hype hooks | Native-looking posts and notes from Richard, the transformation with an honest caption, and member UGC after cohort 1 |
| **Social proof** | "Thousands of men" | Real names, real numbers, a live count |

Native statics and UGC are the right ad formats here. They match the feed's genre ("posts look like posts"), so
the message arrives as a man talking, not as an ad.

## 7. Price is part of the position

Supreme: "don't come in here and tell us your stuff's too expensive, it's not for you." What a man pays tells him what
he's joined.

> **Updated 11 Oct 2026:** for now the price stays at $9, repositioned as "the $9 bet" (worst case vs best case, a
> 35-day completion guarantee). See `08-the-9-dollar-offer.md`. The plan below is kept for a later price test.

**Original decision (before the $9 bet):**

1. **Kill the confusion.** One price, stated the same in the ad, video, page and checkout.
2. **Core offer: a founding cohort at a real price**, with the 30-day guarantee carrying the risk (not a low price).
   Test $97 against $197.
3. **Your "$9 first month" idea runs as the challenger**, structured honestly: $9 for the 35-day Foundation, then the
   Brotherhood at $X a month, with the monthly price shown up front everywhere.
4. **Judge both on what matters:** finish rate, refund rate, members still active at day 60, and qualified calls.
   Not cost per sale alone.
5. **The Brotherhood is earned:** only men who finish the 35 days get in. That's the status ratchet (Module 4).
6. **The strategy call is offered on day 35 to finishers.** Ask about net worth there, not before the first purchase.

## 8. Tension, story, culture

**Tension (real, not stress):**

- Cohorts start on fixed dates ("the November cohort starts Monday 3rd. We start together.").
- Real places tied to a real constraint (Richard reads every man's ten rules and runs a weekly call): "34 of 50 taken",
  live and true.
- The culture existing is tension too. Weekly finisher stories and the rebuild log show men like him doing it.

**The story he tells (Module 6), and the milk carton (Module 4):**

- **The Code card.** His ten rules, on the fridge or the desk. His partner reads it. It says "let's talk about this."
- **Bring a brother.** Each finisher can bring one man (a brother, a business partner, a best mate) into the next
  cohort. It makes him the leader, and it makes his own life better when that man changes.
- **The "nice shoes" moment (TOMS).** The visible change: weight off, phone face down, home for dinner. "What are you
  doing differently?" "Richard's 35 days."

**Culture ("people like us do things like this"):**

- Rituals: the day-1 code, the Sunday scorecard, phones face down at dinner, a monthly morning session.
- Language: training, cohort, code, scorecard, foundation. Not "course" or "self-help".

## 9. Rollout: smallest viable audience first

1. **Founding cohort of 100 from permission.** Invite the men who fit the persona from Richard's property clients and
   leads, YouTube, Instagram and email. Seth started Purple Cow with readers who already trusted him.
2. **Run cohort 1 for proof:** finish rate, before and after, and the men's own words and faces.
3. **Start the rebuild log at the same time,** so the culture is visible.
4. **Then cold ads** built from cohort 1's language, pointing at the next real start date.
5. **High-ticket comes last,** offered to finishers who want more.

## 10. What we measure (and what we ignore)

Module 6 asks us to say out loud what we'll measure and what we'll ignore. Richard's whole message is about false
proxies, so the marketing has to practise it.

| Measure | Why |
|---|---|
| Day-1 completion (the code written) | He's in |
| Day-35 completion rate | The promise kept |
| Members active in the Brotherhood at day 60 and day 90 | Affiliation is real |
| Refund rate | The honesty check |
| Brought-a-brother rate | The network effect |
| Qualified calls per 100 finishers, and close rate | The business |
| **Ignore as goals** (but keep healthy): clicks, CTR, cost per $9 sale, video views | False proxies |

## 11. Promise-breakers to fix (from the funnel audit)

Mohammed will settle the numbers with Richard. The structural fixes:

1. **One price everywhere.** Ads and video say "under $97"; checkout says $9.
2. **One fact sheet** for every number in the story (see `04-richard-story-bank.md`).
3. **One framework** (section 4) instead of four pillar sets and three names for day 1.
4. **Remove the guru-genre lines:**
   - "Might make you angry"
   - "Secrets whispered in back rooms… hidden from ordinary men"
   - "Thousands of men in a growing movement" (the Skool group has 11)
   - "Most men clicked away in 90 seconds"
   - "If you're a simp, don't click"
   - "First 100, then the price goes up / this will be gone"
   - The $5,626 value stack and the price drop-down
5. **Reframe the wealth.** It's real. Show it as a receipt (the setting of the collapse: "a $1.5M view and no one to
   call"), not as bait (the Range Rover and first-class dream list).
6. **The page must deliver what the ads promise:** curriculum, price, guarantee, real proof.
7. **Smaller fixes:**
   - Move the net-worth question off the first purchase.
   - Fix the dead footer links.
   - Turn off the ad that still says "September 1st".
   - Cut or chapter the 25-minute video (about 9% press play; the average viewer watches about 3½ minutes).
