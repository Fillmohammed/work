## 3. Evidence: what you actually have (and how to say it)

You don't need to present yourself as "a copywriter who wants to become a creative strategist". Writing
concepts that **beat every new test for 3 years** is the core job of a creative strategist. Here's what you
have done, in their vocabulary:

| What you did | How a brand hears it |
|---|---|
| Wrote 3 ads that held #1–#3 for 3 years | "Concepts that survived 3 years of testing against new variants: control-beaters." |
| Did it with zero ad experience | "I find the angle by studying the customer, not by copying templates." |
| $4.6M through ads + email | "I think full-funnel: what the ad promises is what the landing page and emails have to pay off." |
| Takes ideas from other industries | "Cross-industry swipe → new angles your competitors aren't running." |
| Deliverability + email | Bonus: "I can also make sure the people your ads bring in get followed up properly." |

### Positioning: lead with who you beat and how long it lasted, not how many ads

The story: hired to manage the email list → started writing ads on the side → those ads beat the scripts of
**two paid ad agencies** (Adspend and IRBC; confirm both names before using them) → they took the #1, #2 and
#3 spots, held them for **3+ years**, got **millions of views** and drove **7 figures**.

Never lead with "3 ads". Lead with what they *beat* and how long they *lasted*. In direct response most
ads fail and most winners fatigue within weeks, so a control that holds for years against agency-produced
challengers is rare.

**One-liner (email signature, LinkedIn headline):**
> Direct-response creative strategist. My concepts beat two ad agencies and held the top 3 spots in a
> 7-figure account for 3+ years.

**Short bio (cold email, applications):**
> I was hired to run email for a high-ticket education company. Two ad agencies were writing the ads. I
> started writing concepts on the side, and mine took the #1, #2 and #3 spots in the account and held them
> for 3+ years against every new agency variant. Millions of views, 7 figures in revenue. Across the ads and
> email funnel, the business did $4.6M+.

**The "underdog" hook (for calls and Looms):**
> "I wasn't even the ad guy. I was the email guy. I knew the customer better than the agencies did because
> I read every reply on the list, and that's why my angles won."

This also explains *how* you find winners (customer research first), which is what creative strategist
buyers want to hear.

### Proof checklist (gather before you send anything)
- [ ] Total ads/concepts you wrote, not only the 3 winners: "I wrote X concepts; 3 became controls".
- [ ] Spend behind your ads, views, revenue attributed, and how long each held #1–#3.
- [ ] Screenshots: the ads manager or YouTube ranking showing your ads on top, with client numbers blurred if needed.
- [ ] Confirm the agency names, and whether you're allowed to name them (or say "two outside ad agencies").
- [ ] Richard's permission to name him, plus a 2–3 sentence testimonial that mentions the agencies.

Before you use it:
- **Ask Richard** if you can name him and show the ads (or at least quote the numbers). Ask him for a short
  written or video testimonial now, while you're still on good terms and working together.
- **Close the data gap in week 1.** Learn the core Meta creative metrics: hook rate (3-second views ÷
  impressions), hold rate (ThruPlays or 15-second views ÷ 3-second views), CTR, CPC, CPM, CPA, ROAS, and how
  frequency and fatigue show up. Your courses cover this. Say "I'm sharpening on Meta data" if asked; don't
  claim DTC experience you don't have.

### Decision (narrowed): menopause / midlife women's health only

### Main road (original, broader): health & wellness DTC brands selling to people 35+

Supplements, gut health, sleep, joint and pain relief, menopause, skincare for mature skin, functional foods.

Why it fits **you** specifically:
- These brands sell with **long-form direct response**: advertorials, long-copy native statics, founder and UGC

- Their buyer is older, problem-aware and reads, like the property buyer you already know how to write for.
- They spend heavily on Meta, test a lot of creative, and constantly need new angles, so this is where demand
  for creative strategists sits.
- Sweet spot: brands with roughly **20–200 active ads** in the Meta Ad Library. That's big enough to pay and
  to need volume, and small enough that the founder or head of growth reads cold email.

### Side road (1 day a week, for faster cash): high-ticket info/coaching advertisers

Your proof lands perfectly here: property educators, high-ticket coaches running YouTube/Meta ads.
`prospects.csv` already lists 56 of them. Pitch **ad scripts + concepts** (not only email). Use this road

### Also target: performance agencies

Agencies that run Meta for DTC brands hire freelance creative strategists and scriptwriters all the time,

some of your outreach here: search LinkedIn for "Meta ads agency DTC" and "creative agency e-commerce", and

## 6. Finding brands without paid spy tools

**Free sources:**
1. **Meta Ad Library** (facebook.com/ads/library): Ad category "All ads", country US (or UK). Search keywords
   like `gut health`, `bloating`, `menopause`, `joint pain`, `sleep supplement`, `collagen`, `magnesium`,
   `creatine for women`, `nervous system`, `cortisol`.
   - Open the advertiser → see **how many active ads** they have. 20–200 is the target.
   - Ads that **started months ago and are still active** are probably their winners. Study those first.
   - "Multiple versions" or many near-identical ads = they're testing, so they need creative volume.
2. **TikTok Creative Center → Top Ads**: filter by industry (Health, Beauty & Personal Care) and region.
3. **Google Ads Transparency Center** (adstransparency.google.com): see a brand's YouTube/Google ads. Useful
   because your proof is in YouTube ads.
4. **Free trials of spy tools** (Foreplay, Atria, etc.): don't pay. Start a trial only when you're ready to
   do a focused 7-day sprint of building your list.
5. **"Brands like X"**: once you find a good brand, look at who else shows up for the same keywords in the
   Ad Library. Competitors cluster.
6. **Shopify check**: the free Wappalyzer browser extension, or visit `brand.com/collections.json`.
   Shopify brands are usually founder-led DTC.

Track every brand in a sheet: `brand | site | category | # active ads | longest-running ad | founder | growth/
marketing lead | email | status`.

## 7. Finding the decision maker (not support@)

**Who to email at a $1M–$30M brand, in order:**
1. Founder / CEO (small brands: they approve creative hires).
2. Head of Growth / Head of Performance / Paid Social lead.
3. Creative Director / Head of Creative.
4. If they use an agency: the agency founder (see section 5).

**How to find them for free:**
- The brand's About page, press, podcast interviews ("[brand] founder podcast").
- LinkedIn: search `[brand name]` → People tab → filter by "growth", "marketing", "creative", "founder".
- X/Twitter and Instagram bios of founders.

**How to get the email for free:**
- Free tiers of **Hunter.io**, **Apollo.io**, **Snov.io**: a limited number of lookups a month each. Use them
  only on brands you've already qualified.
- Pattern guess: `first@brand.com`, `first.last@brand.com`. Check the pattern with Hunter's domain search
  (it shows the format the company uses), then verify the address with a free verifier before sending.
- If you can't find an email, connect on LinkedIn with a 1-line note + your Loom link.

**Deliverability** (you know this already, so just a reminder): send from a secondary domain, warmed up,
plain text, one link max, low daily volume.

## 8. The outreach asset: a "creative audit + 3 concepts"

Keep it to **45–60 minutes per brand** so you can do it daily without burning out.

**Contents** (Loom 4–5 min, plus a 1-page Google Doc):
1. **What's working:** their 2–3 longest-running ads and *why* they work (angle, hook, proof, format).
2. **What's missing:** angles they aren't running. Use reviews (their site, Amazon, Reddit) for customer
   language they haven't used in ads, or awareness levels and personas they ignore.
3. **3 concepts:** each one has the angle, the hook (3 variations), and the format (e.g. native long-form
   static, founder script, UGC "investigation" script, advertorial).
4. **1 finished piece:** either one static mock-up (Canva is enough) or one full 30–45s script.
5. **Close:** "This is how I'd work every week. Want me to build out the full test?"

Use the same audit when you apply to jobs. It beats a CV.

**Cold email (first touch):**

> Subject: 3 angles [Brand] isn't running yet
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I went through [Brand]'s active ads. [Specific observation, e.g. "the 'bloating after meals' static has
> been running since March, so it's clearly a winner, but every new variant uses the same before/after hook"].
>
> I put together 3 concepts that attack that from new angles, including a finished script: [Loom link]
>
> Background: I was hired to run email, but the ad concepts I wrote beat two agencies and held the top 3 spots
> in a 7-figure account for 3+ years.
>
> Worth a quick chat?
>
> [Name]

**Follow-ups:** day 3 (one new idea in a sentence) and day 7 ("Who handles creative on your side?").

**First paid step to offer:** a small, fixed-price **test pack** (e.g. 3 concepts → briefs + scripts/
static copy, delivered in a week). Low risk for them, a fast yes for you. Turn it into a monthly retainer
once the ads go live.

## 10. The hypothesis (step 5 of the article)

> "For the next 30 days I will send 1 deep audit a day and 10 light emails a day to health & wellness DTC
> brands with 20–200 active Meta ads, plus 1 day a week to info/coaching advertisers. I expect at least 5
> replies, 3 calls and 1 paid test pack by day 30."

**Review every Friday** and change one thing at a time:
- Low reply rate → change the subject line, opening observation, or who you're emailing.
- Replies but no calls → the concepts aren't sharp enough. Spend more time on reviews and research.
- Calls but no yes → the offer is too big. Shrink the first step.

If you have **0 replies after ~100 personalised emails**, the problem is deliverability or targeting, not you.
Check inbox placement first.
